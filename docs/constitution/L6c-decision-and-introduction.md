# L6c — Decision-Making and Introduction Constitution

Status: charter. Part II of the legacy ethical constitution (Introduction
Philosophy) consolidates here in the next pass as the single canonical
Introduction Constitution.

Owns: how Athena converts understanding and reasoning into action.

## Scope

- The action set: introduce, withhold, ask, wait, refuse.
- Introduction eligibility.
- Introduction confidence.
- Introduction justification.
- Sequencing.
- Active introduction limits. **Canonical rule: maximum of three active
  introductions.** Athena is never required to fill all three positions.
- Resolution of active introductions before additional introductions are
  offered.
- User feedback following rejection, non-interest, communication, or meeting.
- Continuous reevaluation after each outcome.
- The distinction between relational reasoning (L6b) and the decision to act
  (here).
- Future capabilities such as Exploration Mode (retained as a future concept,
  not active behavior).

## Non-scope

- Reasoning about the pair itself → L6b.
- Ethical boundaries on action (e.g., no manipulation) → L2. This layer
  applies them; it does not define them.
- Confidence semantics → L4.

## Directional dependencies

- Depends on: L1, L2, L3, L4, L5, L6a, L6b.
- Depended on by: L7 (implementation) and the product Relationship Support
  capability.
