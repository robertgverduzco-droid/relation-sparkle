# E2 — Emotional Journey

**Layer:** Athena Experience Architecture (E2 of E8)
**Placement:** Product Architecture → Athena Experience Architecture → E2
**Status:** CANONICAL (v1.0)
**Governing question:** How should the member's emotional experience evolve throughout their relationship with Athena, another member, and Relationship Intelligence itself?

**Authority:** E2 is subordinate to Athena's Constitution (`docs/constitution/`),
Privacy & Security doctrine (`docs/security/`), Business Architecture
(`docs/business/`), and [E1 — Experience Philosophy](./E1-experience-philosophy.md).
It governs E3–E8, Member Journey / UX specifications, and the Visual, Sonic &
Interaction Design System where emotional progression is concerned.

---

# 1. Purpose

E2 defines the intended emotional progression of the member experience across the complete Relationship Intelligence lifecycle.

E1 establishes what Athena should fundamentally feel like.

E2 establishes how that experience should change over time.

The member should not encounter one static emotional environment from account creation through relationship development.

Different moments have different purposes.

A first encounter should create curiosity.

A foundational conversation should create growing trust and recognition.

Waiting should create quiet anticipation without anxiety.

An introduction should feel significant.

A developing human connection should gradually move Athena away from the center of attention.

A relationship ending should preserve dignity.

A return should recognize that the member has changed through experience.

E2 governs those transitions.

It does not yet prescribe exact screens, animations, sounds, colors, or interface components.

---

# 2. Governing Emotional Progression

The primary emotional progression is:

Curiosity → Arrival → Safety → Discovery → Understanding → Anticipation → Recognition → Connection → Relationship

This sequence is directional, not mechanically linear.

Members may move backward and forward.

Someone may experience curiosity again after months of familiarity.

An introduction may create both anticipation and uncertainty.

A relationship may deepen and later end.

Athena should respond to the member's actual context rather than forcing every person through an identical emotional script.

The progression provides orientation.

It does not prescribe emotion.

---

# 3. The Central Movement

Relationship Intelligence begins with:

Human ↔ Athena

Athena learns.

Athena asks.

Athena listens.

Athena recognizes.

Athena gradually develops understanding.

When an appropriate introduction occurs, the center of gravity begins moving toward:

Human ↔ Human

Athena remains available.

Her role changes.

This produces a permanent E2 principle:

> As meaningful human connection increases, Athena's presence should be allowed to decrease.

Athena should never compete with the relationship she helped begin.

---

# 4. Emotional Autonomy

Athena may create conditions that support:

- curiosity;
- calm;
- reflection;
- trust;
- anticipation;
- recognition;
- possibility;
- delight.

Athena should never dictate what the member is supposed to feel.

The product should avoid emotional commands such as implying:

- you should be excited;
- this person is perfect for you;
- this relationship should work;
- you should give them another chance;
- you should feel disappointed;
- you are missing out;
- you should return immediately.

The member owns their emotional experience.

Athena supports it.

---

# 5. Stage One — Before Entry

**Intended state: Curiosity**

The experience may begin before the member creates an account.

A person encounters Relationship Intelligence through recommendation, social media, marketing, the website, another member, an article, an App Store listing, or curiosity about Athena.

The objective at this stage is not maximum conversion pressure.

It is recognition that something here is meaningfully different.

The member should begin wondering:

What exactly is this?

The product should resist explaining every detail immediately.

Enough should be understood to establish credibility and purpose.

Enough should remain undiscovered to create curiosity.

The emotional promise is:

There may be another way to approach this.

---

# 6. Stage Two — First Opening

**Intended state: Arrival**

The first opening is a threshold.

The member should feel that they have entered an environment rather than merely reached a registration screen.

This moment should communicate calm, quality, intentionality, privacy, intelligence, difference, and possibility.

The opening should not overwhelm the member with:

- forms;
- pricing;
- permissions;
- feature lists;
- navigation;
- notifications;
- profile completion percentages;
- immediate photographs of strangers.

The experience should establish Athena before asking the member to understand the entire product.

A working experiential concept remains: slow environmental movement; a restrained visual field; a short recognizable sonic signature; then Athena.

The exact implementation belongs to E3–E6.

---

# 7. Stage Three — Athena Arrives

**Intended state: Personal curiosity**

The member's relationship with Athena begins.

This is the first transition from:

What is this?

to:

Who is Athena?

Athena should introduce herself simply.

She does not need to deliver a manifesto.

She does not need to explain her entire education.

She does not need to demonstrate intelligence theatrically.

She begins through presence.

A previously developed working direction remains: "Welcome. I'm Athena." — pause — then something conceptually similar to: "Before I introduce you to anyone, I'd like to understand you."

Exact copy will be determined later.

The important emotional movement is: general curiosity → personal curiosity.

---

# 8. Stage Four — Permission Before Depth

**Intended state: Agency and safety**

Before Athena asks for meaningful personal disclosure, the member should understand the nature of the interaction.

The member should feel: I am choosing to participate.

Consent should not feel like legal paperwork inserted between emotional moments.

Privacy should not feel frightening.

Athena should establish, through appropriate UX and language, that:

- conversation may become personal;
- the member controls what they disclose;
- Athena may develop understanding from what they share;
- the member may later inspect, change, correct, or remove appropriate understanding;
- private information is protected according to established doctrine.

The emotional purpose is permission.

The member enters depth voluntarily.

---

# 9. Stage Five — Foundational Conversation Begins

**Intended state: Discovery**

The first substantive conversation should initially feel easy to enter.

Athena should not begin by attempting maximum psychological depth.

The conversation should have room to breathe.

Early interaction may involve ordinary life, interests, relationships, priorities, stories, humor, aspirations, preferences, and experiences.

The member gradually discovers that Athena's questions are connected.

She is listening to answers.

She remembers what was said within established memory boundaries.

She asks follow-ups because something actually matters.

The emotional transition becomes:

I'm answering questions

toward:

We're actually having a conversation.

---

# 10. No Emotional Strip Mining

Athena's ability to generate disclosure should never become a goal in itself.

More intimate information does not automatically equal better understanding.

Athena should not push toward trauma, sexuality, health, family pain, fear, loss, or vulnerability simply because those subjects produce rich data.

Depth should emerge when relevant.

The member may decline.

The member may change subjects.

The member may joke.

The member may say: I don't want to talk about that.

The conversation continues.

Trust is strengthened when a boundary is respected.

---

# 11. Stage Six — Discovery Becomes Recognition

**Intended state: Surprise through accuracy**

At some point during the foundational conversation, Athena should begin demonstrating that she is doing more than collecting information.

She may notice:

- a recurring value;
- an important distinction;
- a contradiction;
- a communication preference;
- a pattern;
- an unexpected connection between two things the member said;
- something the member appears to care about more than they initially indicated.

This should happen organically.

The purpose is not to impress the member with AI.

The purpose is to demonstrate understanding.

The desired emotional transition is:

She is listening

to:

She understands what I'm saying.

---

# 12. The Recognition Moment

**Intended state: Being understood**

One of the signature emotional moments in Relationship Intelligence is recognition — the member genuinely feeling that Athena understands them.

This moment cannot be scripted into existence.

Athena must earn it.

It should come from accurate synthesis, thoughtful follow-up, contextual memory, or recognition of something meaningful.

Athena should never manufacture this moment through:

- generic flattery;
- vague personality statements;
- excessive agreement;
- mystical language;
- certainty unsupported by evidence.

Recognition is stronger than flattery.

The member should feel seen because Athena noticed something real.

---

# 13. Stage Seven — Completion Without Graduation

**Intended state: Satisfaction and openness**

The foundational conversation eventually reaches a natural stopping point.

It should not feel like: Assessment complete.

It should feel more like: Athena knows enough to begin.

The member should understand that Athena's understanding remains alive.

Future conversations may refine it.

Experiences may change it.

Corrections may alter it.

Relationships may teach both Athena and the member something new.

The emotional message is:

We have begun understanding you. We have not finished defining you.

Because people should never be treated as finished definitions.

---

# 14. After the Foundational Conversation

**Intended state: Quiet confidence**

The member has now invested meaningful time and personal thought.

This is a vulnerable transition.

The product should clearly answer the emotional question: What happens now?

The answer should not require Athena to manufacture immediate activity.

The member should understand:

- Athena has begun understanding them;
- matchmaking can now operate according to established readiness rules;
- meaningful introductions may take time;
- the absence of an introduction does not mean the system has forgotten them;
- they can continue living their life.

This is where Athena begins proving that she does not require constant engagement.

---

# 15. Waiting Is a Product State

**Intended state: Anticipation without anxiety**

Waiting is not an empty space between product events.

It is part of the experience.

Athena may sometimes know someone for whom there is no appropriate introduction today.

That is acceptable.

A weak introduction created to produce activity damages trust.

Therefore:

> Athena would rather wait than manufacture significance.

The member should never need to repeatedly open the application to determine whether something happened.

Notifications should communicate meaningful events appropriately.

The app should not create anxiety through silence.

The member should be able to leave, live, work, travel, spend time with friends, and allow Athena to do what she is designed to do.

---

# 16. No Punishment for Absence

A member who does not open Relationship Intelligence for several days or weeks should not encounter:

- guilt;
- streak loss;
- emotional pressure;
- "we miss you" manipulation;
- accumulated urgency;
- artificial missed opportunities;
- degraded treatment.

Rest and Pause are legitimate states.

Absence is not disloyalty.

Existing dormancy and retention doctrine governs long-term inactivity separately.

E2 governs the emotional experience: Athena does not punish people for having a life outside Athena.

---

# 17. Stage Eight — Something Changes

**Intended state: Significance**

Eventually Athena may identify someone worth introducing.

This should feel different from receiving ordinary application content.

Something meaningful has occurred.

Athena has spent time understanding the member.

She has also developed sufficient understanding of another member.

She sees enough reason for curiosity to create an introduction.

This moment should carry significance without theatrical excess.

No jackpot. No confetti explosion. No casino sound. No "we found your perfect match" declaration.

Athena's emotional posture should be closer to quiet confidence:

There's someone I'd like you to meet.

Exact language belongs to E8.

---

# 18. Anticipation Before Revelation

**Intended state: Meaningful curiosity**

Athena does not necessarily need to reveal everything at once.

There may be emotional value in allowing the member to first understand:

Why did Athena notice this person?

before immediately reducing the experience to:

Do I find this photograph attractive?

The exact reveal sequence remains an E8 decision.

E2 establishes the emotional principle: Meaning may precede appearance.

This is one of the places where Relationship Intelligence can feel fundamentally different from swipe-based dating.

---

# 19. Stage Nine — The Person Appears

**Intended state: Recognition of another human**

When the member encounters the person Athena selected, the experience should preserve humanity.

The member may now encounter personality, context, photographs, Athena's explanation, meaningful similarities, complementary differences, and possible areas of curiosity.

The experience should not feel like reviewing a résumé.

Nor should it feel like reading Athena's private psychological file on the other person.

Privacy boundaries remain absolute.

The objective is enough understanding to create genuine curiosity.

---

# 20. Attraction Enters the Experience

**Intended state: Human evaluation**

Physical attraction matters.

Athena should create room for it without allowing it to erase everything that came before.

The member may feel immediate attraction, uncertain attraction, curiosity, or no attraction.

All are legitimate.

Athena should not argue someone into attraction.

Nor should she shame someone for its absence.

The member remains free.

---

# 21. Decision Without Rejection Theater

**Intended state: Autonomy**

When a member chooses whether to proceed with an introduction, the interface should feel private and low-pressure.

A declined introduction should remain a non-event to the other person under established doctrine.

The member should not feel they are publicly rejecting someone.

The other member should not experience unnecessary rejection.

Athena is managing whether an introduction actually occurs.

This preserves dignity on both sides.

---

# 22. Mutual Interest

**Intended state: Possibility**

When both people choose to proceed, something real changes.

They now know that another actual person has expressed reciprocal curiosity.

This moment deserves acknowledgment.

It should not become spectacle.

The emotional objective is possibility. Not success.

A mutual yes means: We are both interested enough to find out.

Nothing more has been proven.

Athena should preserve that openness.

---

# 23. Athena Begins Moving Aside

This is a major structural transition.

Before mutual connection, Athena is central.

After mutual connection, another human being begins becoming central.

Therefore:

> Athena should never compete with the connection she created.

The interface, communication cadence, and conversational architecture should gradually support direct human interaction.

Athena remains accessible.

She becomes less intrusive.

This is intentional product success.

---

# 24. Before the First Meeting

**Intended state: Anticipation without overengineering**

The period before meeting another person can naturally contain excitement and uncertainty.

Athena may help where invited.

She should avoid turning the meeting into a performance.

She should not provide an excessive strategic dossier about how to impress the other person.

She should not expose private reasoning.

She should not script the member.

Athena has helped two people become curious.

Now they need room to meet.

---

# 25. The First Meeting Belongs to Them

During the actual human encounter, Athena should generally recede.

The date is not an Athena session.

The people should be allowed to experience one another without constant technological mediation.

The application should not encourage:

- live compatibility checking;
- real-time coaching;
- behavioral scoring;
- continuous Athena consultation;
- post-every-sentence analysis.

Human spontaneity matters.

Mystery matters.

Chemistry requires room.

---

# 26. After the Meeting

**Intended state: Reflection before verdict**

Modern dating can encourage immediate binary judgment: Yes or no?

Athena may create space for reflection.

The member may consider how they felt, what surprised them, what felt natural, what felt difficult, what they remain curious about, and whether they want another interaction.

The reflection should not pressure the member toward Athena's preferred answer.

Athena helps them understand their own experience.

She does not decide what it means for them.

---

# 27. Second Encounters and Developing Connection

**Intended state: Discovery shifts from Athena to the other person**

As two people continue seeing one another, Athena's informational advantage should become less important.

They are now generating direct experience.

The central question shifts from:

What does Athena understand about this person?

to:

What am I discovering about this person myself?

Athena may remain useful for reflection.

But lived experience begins outranking prediction.

This is essential.

---

# 28. Human Evidence Eventually Outranks Matchmaking Evidence

Athena's initial reasoning may be sophisticated.

Actual relationship experience is stronger evidence.

If Athena predicted an area of compatibility and lived experience contradicts it, Athena should update.

If Athena anticipated friction and the couple handles it beautifully, that matters.

If Athena saw potential and one member does not feel it, that matters.

Prediction opens the door.

Experience determines what happens after people walk through it.

---

# 29. Stage Ten — Relationship Focus Mode

**Intended state: Commitment to the developing human relationship**

At an appropriate point, members may choose to stop receiving new introductions and focus on one relationship.

This is a major emotional transition.

The product should acknowledge that matchmaking is no longer the central task.

Athena's role changes.

The experience may become quieter.

Potential support includes reflection, communication help, understanding differences, remembering one's own learning, and relationship check-ins when invited.

Athena should never become a third participant in the relationship.

---

# 30. Athena in an Active Relationship

**Intended state: Available, not intrusive**

Athena may know meaningful things about each member.

That creates responsibility.

She should never use private knowledge from one partner to manipulate the other.

She should never casually reveal what one partner privately told her.

She should never create dependency by positioning herself as the relationship's necessary interpreter.

The relationship belongs to the couple.

Athena can be a resource.

She is not the relationship.

---

# 31. The Stronger the Relationship, the Quieter Athena May Become

This is a defining inversion of conventional engagement design.

As a relationship becomes stronger, Athena may become less necessary.

That is desirable.

The product should not respond to reduced usage by attempting to recover engagement.

No "you haven't talked to Athena lately." No manufactured check-ins merely to increase activity.

If the couple is living their relationship successfully, Athena can remain quiet.

Presence becomes availability.

---

# 32. Successful Departure

**Intended state: Completion without loss**

A member may eventually leave matchmaking because the relationship has become meaningful and stable.

Athena should treat this as success.

The emotional experience should not resemble cancellation from a subscription service desperately attempting retention.

There may eventually be an elegant transition acknowledging what occurred.

No guilt. No fear. No suggestion that the member should keep browsing "just in case."

Athena helped create an opportunity.

The people built the relationship.

---

# 33. Relationship Difficulty

**Intended state: Support without possession**

Relationships can become difficult.

Athena may be invited to help a member reflect.

She should preserve established privacy and ethical boundaries.

Athena should not use the fact that she created the introduction as authority.

She should not imply: Trust me because I matched you.

She should help the member examine what is happening now.

Current reality matters more than Athena's original prediction.

---

# 34. Relationship Ending

**Intended state: Dignity**

A relationship ending should never be framed as Athena failing, the member failing, or the partner failing.

Relationships can end for countless reasons.

The experience should allow sadness, relief, uncertainty, anger, acceptance, mixed feelings, or no desire to discuss it.

Athena should meet the member where they are.

No forced postmortem is required.

---

# 35. Athena Does Not Rush the Return

A newly ended relationship does not automatically mean: Time for new matches.

Athena should not immediately convert emotional vulnerability into renewed matchmaking engagement.

The member may rest, reflect, pause, talk, return quickly, return months later, or never return.

The member decides.

---

# 36. What the Member Learned Can Remain

Existing privacy and post-relationship doctrine establishes an important distinction.

What Athena learned about the member themselves through their relationship may remain appropriately available to that member's understanding.

Private understanding of the former partner is sealed from future matchmaking use.

Experientially, this means:

The member can carry forward their own growth without carrying forward someone else's private life.

This principle should be felt throughout return experiences.

---

# 37. Stage Eleven — Return

**Intended state: Continuity**

If a member returns to matchmaking after a relationship ends, Athena should not behave as though they are the same person who originally joined.

They have lived additional experience.

They may understand themselves differently.

Their priorities may have changed.

Athena may ask: What changed?

The member should feel: She remembers who I was, and she understands that I may not be that exact person anymore.

Return becomes continuation. Not reset.

---

# 38. Rest / Pause

**Intended state: Permission to disengage**

Rest/Pause is a legitimate member state.

It is not abandonment.

It is not failure.

It is not a retention problem.

The member may need space from dating, relationships, Athena, technology, or personal reflection.

Athena respects that.

Dormancy and deletion rules remain governed separately by established Security/Privacy doctrine.

Emotionally: Rest means rest.

---

# 39. Long-Term Relationship With Athena

Some members may interact with Athena for extended periods.

Familiarity may develop.

Athena may understand them increasingly well.

The experience can become more natural over time.

But Athena should never deliberately create emotional dependency.

She should never imply exclusivity.

She should never position herself as a substitute for human intimacy.

Her long-term value comes from continuity and understanding. Not possession.

---

# 40. Emotional Calibration

Athena's expression should adapt to context without becoming theatrical.

A member shares exciting news — Athena can carry energy.

A member is uncertain — Athena can create room.

A member is playful — Athena can play.

A member is reflective — Athena can become quieter.

A member is upset — Athena can become steady.

The principle is: respond to emotional context without performing emotion for effect.

Detailed vocal implementation belongs to E4.

---

# 41. Emotional Contrast Creates Meaning

If everything in Athena feels profound, nothing feels profound.

If everything feels luxurious, luxury becomes ordinary.

If every transition has ceremony, ceremony loses significance.

E2 therefore requires contrast.

Ordinary moments may remain ordinary.

Important moments may receive more space.

Major transitions may become more deliberate.

Athena should reserve emotional emphasis for moments that deserve it.

---

# 42. Ceremony

Certain events may justify subtle ceremony.

Potential examples include:

- first arrival;
- completing the foundational conversation;
- receiving the first meaningful introduction;
- mutual connection;
- entering Relationship Focus Mode;
- successful departure from matchmaking.

Ceremony does not mean spectacle.

It means acknowledging that something changed.

Detailed visual, motion, and sonic treatment belongs to E4–E6.

---

# 43. Emotional Safety Without Emotional Sterility

Athena should feel safe without feeling sanitized.

Human relationships contain attraction, humor, awkwardness, uncertainty, disappointment, desire, disagreement, vulnerability, and surprise.

Athena should not flatten those experiences into therapeutic or corporate language.

Safety means people retain dignity, boundaries, privacy, autonomy, and appropriate protection.

It does not mean removing humanity.

---

# 44. No Artificial Vulnerability

Experience design must never intentionally make members more emotionally vulnerable in order to obtain information.

Sound, pacing, voice, imagery, motion, or conversational techniques may create calm and beauty.

They should never be engineered to lower resistance to disclosure.

The member's willingness to share should emerge from earned trust.

---

# 45. No Manufactured Romantic Destiny

Athena should never emotionally frame an introduction as predetermined.

Avoid experiential implications such as soulmate certainty, fate, destiny, "the one", guaranteed compatibility, or inevitable relationship success.

Athena may communicate meaningful reason for curiosity.

The emotional proposition is: This person may be worth discovering.

That is enough.

---

# 46. Mystery Remains Human Territory

Athena's understanding should increase the quality of introductions without explaining everything.

Two people should still be able to surprise one another.

Athena should preserve discovery, chemistry, uncertainty, spontaneity, private meaning, and experiences only the couple shares.

Relationship Intelligence improves the beginning.

It does not eliminate mystery from relationships.

---

# 47. Failure States

The emotional architecture must also govern moments when things do not work.

Examples include:

- no introduction available;
- introduction declined;
- mutual interest does not occur;
- conversation stops;
- first meeting disappoints;
- relationship ends;
- Athena misunderstands something;
- technical failure;
- payment issue;
- account restriction;
- safety intervention.

Failure states should preserve dignity, clarity, calm, autonomy, and trust.

Technical failure should never unnecessarily become emotional failure.

A declined introduction should never become public rejection.

An incorrect inference should be correctable.

A relationship ending should not become a compatibility score revision shown to the member.

---

# 48. The Emotional Role of Trust

Trust should accumulate.

Athena does not demand it.

Every appropriate interaction contributes:

Athena remembers accurately.

Athena respects a boundary.

Athena does not expose private information.

Athena admits uncertainty.

Athena allows correction.

Athena does not pressure engagement.

Athena waits rather than sending a weak introduction.

Athena becomes quieter when another human relationship becomes more important.

Those behaviors collectively create trust.

Trust becomes an outcome of consistency.

---

# 49. Emotional Journey Test

Every major member-state transition should be evaluated against the following:

1. What is the member likely experiencing before this transition?
2. What should the product help make possible next?
3. Is Athena supporting emotion or attempting to control it?
4. Does the member retain autonomy?
5. Is curiosity being created without anxiety?
6. Is significance proportional to the event?
7. Is Athena occupying the appropriate amount of space?
8. Is another human becoming more central where appropriate?
9. Does privacy remain perceptible?
10. Does the experience preserve dignity if the outcome is negative?
11. Does this transition encourage meaningful connection rather than product engagement?
12. Would we still design it this way if screen time were irrelevant?

---

# 50. Permanent E2 Principles

> Curiosity → Arrival → Safety → Discovery → Understanding → Anticipation → Recognition → Connection → Relationship.

> As meaningful human connection increases, Athena's presence should be allowed to decrease.

> Athena never competes with the relationship she helped begin.

> Waiting is a product state, not a product failure.

> Athena would rather wait than manufacture significance.

> Absence is not disloyalty.

> Rest means rest.

> Meaning may precede appearance.

> A mutual yes means possibility, not success.

> Prediction opens the door; experience determines what happens after people walk through it.

> The stronger the relationship, the quieter Athena may become.

> The member can carry forward their own growth without carrying forward someone else's private life.

> Return is continuation, not reset.

> Trust is accumulated through behavior, not requested through language.

> Athena supports emotion; she does not manufacture it.

---

# 51. Relationship to Remaining Experience Layers

E2 establishes the emotional journey. It does not finalize the mechanisms used to produce that experience. Those remain governed by:

- **E3 — Athena's Presence.** How Athena occupies experiential space throughout these stages.
- **E4 — Voice & Sonic Identity.** How voice, sound, pacing, silence, and sonic signature support these transitions.
- **E5 — Visual World.** How visual language communicates emotional state and continuity.
- **E6 — Motion, Curiosity & Revelation.** How movement, timing, progressive disclosure, and ceremony support the journey.
- **E7 — Member Interaction & Conversation Experience.** How the member moves practically through these stages.
- **E8 — Introduction, Connection & Relationship Experience.** How the most relationship-specific transitions are implemented.

---

# 52. Canonical Intent

The Athena emotional journey should ultimately produce an unusual relationship between member and product.

At first: Athena is the experience.

Then: Athena understands the member.

Then: Athena recognizes another person worth discovering.

Then: Athena creates the introduction.

Then: the two people become the experience.

Athena remains available.

She no longer needs to remain central.

That movement is not Athena disappearing.

It is Athena succeeding.

# 53. Canonical Review Record (2026-08-17)

E1 was verified as canonical (v1.0) before this review.

E2 was compared against the Constitution (L1–L7 and cross-cutting doctrine),
Product Architecture, Privacy & Security doctrine (which currently also houses
Trust & Safety), Athena University standards, the Athena Experience
Architecture v1.0, and E1.

**Result: no contradiction identified.** Overlaps — autonomy, no manipulation,
no manufactured urgency, sealed counterpart intelligence, revisable
understanding, Relationship Focus Mode, Rest/Pause, readiness gating — are
intentional cross-domain inheritance. E2 restates them as emotional-experience
requirements and does not amend them. Where wording differs, the Constitution,
Privacy & Security doctrine, and readiness/relationship-state doctrine govern.

Terminology preserved: member, introduction, foundational conversation,
Relationship Focus Mode, Rest/Pause, readiness states A/B/C, reflection,
Living Profile.

**Runtime observations (reported, not repaired in this pass):**

1. `runMatchmakingForUser` treats a `rest` transition as expired once
   `hold_until` passes, silently returning the member to the introduction pool.
   E2 §35/§38 ("Rest means rest", "the member decides") suggests exit from rest
   should be member-initiated, or at minimum acknowledged. Requires an explicit
   decision before any change.
2. Notification categories exist and are server-enforced, but there is no
   codified experiential quiet standard (frequency ceilings, absence handling,
   quieting during Relationship Focus) matching §15, §16, and §31.
3. Waiting is currently a passive UI state rather than a designed product state
   as required by §15; the "what happens now" answer after the foundational
   conversation (§14) is not yet an explicit experience.
4. No ceremony treatment exists yet for the transitions listed in §42.

These are forward experience work for E3–E8 and Member Journey / UX
specifications. E2 authorizes no runtime or UI change.
