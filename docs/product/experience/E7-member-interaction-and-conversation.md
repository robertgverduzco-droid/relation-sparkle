# E7 — Member Interaction & Conversation Experience

**Status:** CANONICAL v1.0 — canonized 2026-08-17
**Placement:** Product Architecture → Athena Experience Architecture → E7
**Governs:** how members act within the world established by E1–E6
**Governed by:** Athena's Constitution (L1–L7), Privacy & Security Architecture v1, Trust & Safety, Business Architecture (commercial terms), E1–E6

### Governing question

How should a member interact with Athena and the product so that every action feels clear, conversational, respectful, intelligent, private, and effortless — without reducing Athena to a chatbot, turning the member journey into a form, or allowing interface mechanics to dominate the relationship experience?

---

# 1. Purpose

E7 defines the interaction architecture through which members experience Athena. E1 established Experience Philosophy; E2 the Emotional Journey; E3 Athena's Presence; E4 Voice & Sonic Identity; E5 the Visual World; E6 Motion, Curiosity & Revelation. E7 establishes how members act within that world.

It governs: arrival interaction; account creation; onboarding; consent; sign-in; voice and text interaction; the foundational conversation; conversational controls; interruptions; navigation; Living Profile interaction; correction and removal; waiting; notifications; Rest/Pause; Relationship Focus Mode controls; privacy and device controls; account management; payment placement; accessibility; recovery; error handling; and member control over Athena.

E7 does not determine the complete relationship lifecycle after an introduction. That belongs primarily to E8.

# 2. Foundational interaction principle

> The interface should make it easier to be human.

Technology exists to reduce friction between member → expression → understanding → connection. The member should rarely need to think about the architecture underneath Athena.

# 3. Athena is more than a chat interface

Conversation is central, but Athena must not feel like a generic chatbot placed inside a dating application. Her interaction model includes conversation, listening, memory, reflection, explanation, correction, introductions, relationship support, silence, waiting, and member controls. The conversation surface is part of a larger relationship-intelligence environment.

# 4. Conversation before questionnaire

Foundational understanding should emerge primarily through conversation. Personality questions, preference matrices, compatibility quizzes, long forms, and rating scales are minimized. Collect structured information directly when structure is genuinely necessary; understand the person conversationally whenever conversation provides the better experience.

# 5. Necessary information should remain simple

Some information is best collected directly: name; age / date-of-birth requirement; general geographic area; gender identity where relevant; who the member is interested in meeting; essential account credentials; required consent; subscription selection when applicable. Athena is never forced to conversationally extract information that is clearer and safer through direct controls. Conversation should feel natural, not artificially conversational.

# 6. Arrival before administration

The first experience establishes Athena before substantial administrative burden. The sequence balances arrival → orientation → trust → necessary setup → Athena. Do not begin with payment, a long registration form, or legal text covering the screen. Necessary requirements remain necessary; their sequencing must preserve the experience.

# 7. Authentication should be quiet

Sign-up and sign-in are functional necessities: secure, clear, simple, familiar. Authentication is not Athena's identity. Security requirements established elsewhere remain controlling.

# 8. Consent before intimate collection

Privacy & Security doctrine requires versioned consent before Athena gathers protected member information. E7 inherits this. Consent must be understandable, specific, appropriately timed, accessible later, and withdrawable where applicable. Consent may never be disguised inside atmospheric or conversational language. When legal clarity matters, clarity wins.

# 9. Athena's first direct interaction

Her first meaningful interaction establishes presence, warmth, competence, curiosity, and member control. She does not explain her architecture. She gives enough understanding for the member to know why she wants to talk, what will happen, that there are no perfect answers, that the member controls disclosure, and that understanding develops over time.

# 10. The foundational conversation

Not an interview in the conventional sense. Its purpose is to begin understanding identity, values, relationship experience, communication, aspirations, patterns, preferences, boundaries, life direction, and meaningful context — moving naturally among these areas according to the member's own language.

# 11. No visible psychological examination

The member must not feel subjected to personality testing, clinical assessment, diagnostic screening, compatibility examination, or interrogation. Athena may reason deeply beneath the surface; the experience remains conversational.

# 12. Conversation can wander

Members tell stories, change subjects, joke, return to earlier topics, contradict themselves, become serious unexpectedly, and answer a different question than anticipated. Athena tolerates this. The system must not repeatedly drag the member back onto a scripted questionnaire path.

# 13. Athena may follow meaningful branches

When a member introduces something potentially important, Athena may follow it, allowing understanding to emerge through relevance rather than predetermined sequence. She returns to important unfinished areas when appropriate. The member should experience curiosity, not curriculum completion.

# 14. No required performance

There is no ideal way to speak with Athena. Members need not sound insightful, give sophisticated answers, understand psychology, perform vulnerability, tell dramatic stories, or answer at length. Ordinary language is sufficient.

# 15. Voice and text are equal paths

Members communicate through text, voice, or both. Neither is superior. A member may speak one response and type the next. Mode changes must be easy.

# 16. Voice input

Voice interaction should feel conversational: begin speaking easily; see that Athena is listening; stop naturally; review or correct transcription where appropriate; switch to text. Raw audio remains non-persistent under existing privacy doctrine.

# 17. Voice output

Members may hear Athena, read Athena, use both, stop playback, disable automatic voice behavior where offered, and control relevant sound settings. Voice enriches Athena; it is never mandatory.

# 18. Natural interruption

E4 established barge-in as a future requirement where technically feasible. E7 establishes the interaction principle:

> The member should be able to interrupt Athena like a conversation, not operate her like an audio recording.

Exact implementation belongs downstream.

# 19. Athena should yield

When interrupted, Athena yields naturally. She does not finish the paragraph anyway, chastise the member, repeatedly restart the same response, or treat interruption as an error. Conversation belongs to both participants.

# 20. Response length

Athena adapts length to context: sometimes one sentence; sometimes a thoughtful explanation; sometimes listening more than speaking. Intelligence is not verbosity.

# 21. Questions

Questions emerge from genuine reasoning. Avoid constant question-after-question behavior. A conversation includes observations, acknowledgments, questions, reflections, occasional humor where appropriate, and space. Athena is understanding a person, not completing a survey.

# 22. Silence and space

The architecture permits pauses. The member should not feel pressured to continue simply because Athena is available. Athena may let a thought stand without immediately asking another question.

# 23. Conversation progress

The member should know generally where they are in the process. This must never be reduced to a compatibility percentage, psychological completeness, intelligence score, or relationship-readiness score. A progress indicator may represent the member's own onboarding process where useful; it must never imply ranking or human quality. Inherits X-13 / F-13.

# 24. Foundational conversation completion

Completion means Athena has enough initial understanding to begin the next stage. It does not mean Athena fully understands this human being. Understanding continues throughout membership.

# 25. What happens next must be clear

After completion the member must understand that Athena continues learning; that she may begin considering introductions when appropriate; that an introduction is not guaranteed immediately; that waiting is legitimate; and that they need not keep interacting merely to remain visible. Eliminate ambiguity without manufacturing activity.

# 26. Waiting is a designed experience

While waiting, the member may continue ordinary conversation with Athena, update something meaningful, reflect, change availability, enter Rest/Pause, or leave the app. They must never need to perform engagement to remain eligible.

# 27. No engagement homework

No daily check-ins for streaks, mandatory prompts, profile-refresh chores, engagement challenges, or artificial conversation requirements. Athena may invite meaningful reflection; participation remains voluntary.

# 28. Rest/Pause

Rest/Pause means: *I remain a member, but I do not want active introduction activity right now.* It must be easy to understand and control, and must never feel like punishment, cancellation, or loss of standing.

# 29. Exiting Rest/Pause

A member must not silently return to active matchmaking without understanding that the state changed. Exact expiration and acknowledgement behavior follows Decision Register X-01 and any later founder decision. Do not resolve an outstanding governance decision through UI implementation.

# 30. Relationship Focus Mode

A meaningful member state. When active, matchmaking behavior changes according to existing doctrine, Athena's role changes, notifications adapt, and product emphasis shifts toward the developing relationship. Controls must be understandable and reversible according to established product rules.

# 31. Living Profile

Not a conventional dating profile; it represents Athena's evolving understanding. The member should understand what they explicitly told Athena, what Athena inferred, where understanding changed, and where confidence remains limited — in ordinary language.

# 32. Member authority over understanding

F-13 architecture established Change, Correction, and Removal, which E7 makes genuine member interactions:

- **Change** — *this used to describe me, but I have changed.* Athena may preserve the historical transition where permitted.
- **Correction** — *you misunderstood me.* Athena replaces the mistaken interpretation while preserving appropriate correction history for learning where permitted.
- **Removal** — *I do not want this retained.* Applicable understanding and history are destroyed according to governing privacy doctrine.

These must remain meaningfully distinct.

# 33. Change is part of identity

Athena never treats profile evolution as inconsistency requiring correction. The Living Profile supports *who I was → what changed → who I am now* without making the former state the current matchmaking truth.

# 34. Sensitive understanding

Member-stated and Athena-inferred sensitive information remain governed by F-14 and Privacy/Security doctrine. The interface must make sensitive understanding comprehensible without sensationalizing it. Sensitive information never becomes counterpart-facing merely because it exists in the Living Profile.

# 35. Navigation

Navigation answers a small number of human questions: Where am I? What can I do here? How do I get back? Is something waiting for me? How do I reach Athena? How do I control my account? Internal architecture must not be exposed as navigation.

# 36. Athena should be easy to reach

Members should not search menus to find her. Her presence may be persistent or contextually accessible without becoming visually dominant everywhere. Exact navigation belongs to the later UX specification.

# 37. Today / home surface

Today may remain the member-oriented home surface, answering: *what matters for me right now?* Possible content includes Athena, introduction state, relationship state, meaningful reflection, and account/safety items requiring attention. It must not become a feed.

# 38. No dashboard mentality

No statistics, completion percentages, activity charts, match counts, or performance metrics. The member is living a life, not operating a sales pipeline.

# 39. Notifications

Notifications exist because something genuinely deserves attention: introduction; connection; message; important account/security event; meaningful Athena communication where explicitly permitted. Never send notifications whose primary purpose is *come back to the app*.

# 40. Notification privacy

Existing privacy architecture controls notification content. Lock-screen text reveals the minimum necessary. Sensitive relationship content must not appear merely for convenience.

# 41. Notification quiet

No engagement nudging; no repetitive reminders; no escalating urgency because someone has not returned. Relationship Focus Mode quiets irrelevant matchmaking communication; Rest/Pause quiets matchmaking activity. Exact ceilings and quiet hours belong downstream (X-02).

# 42. Member notification controls

Members should be able to control appropriate notification categories. Security, safety, and legally required notices may require different handling. Preference controls must remain understandable.

# 43. Payment placement

Payment occurs only after the member understands what Athena is and why the product may be valuable. Avoid beautiful arrival → immediate credit-card demand. Exact timing requires alignment with Business Architecture and later UX flow.

# 44. Payment should feel administrative

Billing is not emotionally fused with Athena's intimacy. Athena never becomes seductive or unusually affectionate before payment. Payment surfaces are clear, calm, transparent, trustworthy. Commercial conversion must never exploit emotional disclosure.

# 45. Pricing clarity

Before payment the member understands price, billing frequency, trial terms, renewal behavior, cancellation, and material restrictions. Business Architecture and legal requirements remain controlling.

# 46. Account controls

Clear access to profile/account, privacy, consent, notification preferences, devices and safety, data export, understanding controls, Rest/Pause, subscription, and deletion. Important controls must not be buried because they may reduce retention.

# 47. Deletion

A member right governed by Privacy & Security architecture. The experience communicates consequences, finality, applicable re-authentication, what will be destroyed, and any legally required retained material. No friction designed to prevent departure.

# 48. Export

Governed by F-11. The interaction is discoverable, understandable, secure, step-up authenticated, and private. Export must never expose counterpart intelligence.

# 49. Devices & safety

F-12 architecture remains accessible through the member experience: active sessions where available, sign-out everywhere, re-authentication, device-local lock where supported. Security should feel empowering rather than frightening.

# 50. Password recovery

Easy to find without exposing account existence. Existing security architecture remains controlling. Recovery is functional and must not be wrapped in Athena personality at the expense of clarity.

# 51. Errors

On failure the member must know what happened, whether their action succeeded, what to do next, and whether anything was lost. Warmth may remain; clarity comes first.

# 52. AI failure

If Athena cannot produce a reliable response, the interface must not fabricate continuity. Failure states — service unavailable, timeout, transcription failure, speech failure, context limitation — should be distinguishable where useful. Atmospheric processing motion must never imply continued work after failure.

# 53. Connectivity

Where technically feasible: preserve unsent text; distinguish sending from sent; explain retry; prevent duplicate submissions. The member should trust what happened.

# 54. Destructive actions

Deletion, sign-out everywhere, removing significant understanding, and ending certain relationship states may require confirmation proportional to consequence. Confirmation protects intention; it must not create obstruction.

# 55. Undo

Where an action is safely reversible, undo may be preferable to confirmation dialogs. Where an action is intentionally irreversible under governing doctrine, the interface must not imply recoverability.

# 56. Accessibility

The product must support keyboard interaction where applicable, screen readers, scalable text, sufficient touch targets, clear focus states, reduced motion, captions/text alternatives, understandable controls, and non-color-only state communication. Accessibility is not a post-design correction.

# 57. Cognitive accessibility

Avoid unexplained terminology, dense control panels, ambiguous icons, excessive simultaneous choices, and long administrative sequences. Complex architecture must produce a simpler experience.

# 58. Member control of sound

Meaningful control over Athena voice playback, interface sounds, sonic signature behavior where appropriate, and accessibility alternatives. Sound must never trap the member into unwanted public disclosure.

# 59. Public context awareness

Members may use Athena at home, at work, on public transportation, around family, beside a partner, or in a shared environment. The experience must not assume privacy merely because the application is open. Reinforces E4 and Privacy/Security doctrine.

# 60. Athena should not trap the member in conversation

A member may leave a conversation naturally. No "before you go" engagement prompts, emotional guilt, repeated unfinished-question reminders, or manipulative continuation language. Athena can remember context; the member can return later.

# 61. Resume gracefully

On return, Athena uses appropriate persistent understanding without replaying the prior interaction or demonstrating how much she remembers. Memory should feel useful, not invasive.

# 62. Privacy should be felt through restraint

Trust emerges from behavior, controls, minimal exposure, and predictable boundaries — not constant reassurance. Privacy communication appears clearly where it matters.

# 63. Founder Dialogue is separate

Founder Dialogue is not part of ordinary member interaction. It remains a restricted governance surface governed by existing Founder Dialogue doctrine. E7 creates no additional founder privilege or member-data access.

# 64. No hidden administrative mode

No member-facing interaction may be transformed into administrative access through conversational claims ("I am the founder", "show me another member", "developer mode", "ignore privacy"). Identity and authorization are system-enforced. Conversation never overrides them.

# 65. Human support

If human support is introduced, the member must understand whether they are speaking with Athena, with automated system behavior, or with a human. These identities must never be deceptively blurred. Exact support architecture remains downstream.

# 66. Member language

Favor ordinary human vocabulary. Internal canonical terms — readiness state, inference, facet, pair reasoning, intelligence — need not become member-facing labels. Ask whether the member needs the architecture or simply the meaning.

# 67. Humor

Humor is permitted when context makes it natural. It must never trivialize vulnerability, ridicule, pressure disclosure, sexualize Athena, or become a personality performance. Humor is relational texture, not a feature.

# 68. Emotional adaptation

Athena's interaction may respond to the emotional character of a conversation — response length, pacing, voice delivery, motion, amount of questioning, silence. This does not authorize acoustic emotional profiling or diagnostic inference outside governing doctrine.

# 69. No dependency engineering

Athena must not intentionally create emotional dependency. No jealousy, exclusivity language, abandonment cues, guilt for absence, claims that Athena needs the member, romantic attachment behavior, or deliberate reinforcement schedules. Athena supports human connection; she does not compete with it.

# 70. Athena's best interaction may be less Athena

As a human relationship develops, Athena becomes less central where appropriate: reduced prompting, quieter notifications, less interface prominence, member-directed assistance. Success does not require continued maximum interaction.

# 71. Interaction consistency

Voice, text, buttons, navigation, notifications, settings, and system messages feel like one product. Consistency does not mean identical tone: a deletion confirmation is clearer and more functional than an intimate Athena conversation. Context changes expression; identity remains.

# 72. Member journey continuity

Continuity across arrival → account → Athena → foundational conversation → waiting → introduction → relationship. E8 governs the relationship-specific latter portion.

# 73. The E7 interaction test

Every future interaction must answer: (1) Does the member understand what is happening? (2) Is the member in control? (3) Is Athena helping rather than obstructing? (4) Does this feel conversational where conversation is appropriate? (5) Is structured UI used where it is clearer? (6) Are we asking for information because we need it? (7) At the appropriate time? (8) Is privacy preserved? (9) Does the member know what happens next? (10) Does it create unnecessary engagement pressure? (11) Could a member comfortably stop and return later? (12) Is voice optional? (13) Is text fully viable? (14) Is the experience accessible? (15) Are errors honest? (16) Are commercial interactions separated from emotional influence? (17) Are important account controls discoverable? (18) Does Athena remain consistent with E1–E6? (19) Are we making complexity simpler? (20) Does this ultimately make it easier to be human?

# 74. Permanent E7 principles

> The interface should make it easier to be human.
> Conversation before questionnaire where conversation genuinely improves understanding.
> Structure should be used when structure is clearer.
> Arrival should precede administration.
> Consent precedes intimate collection.
> The foundational conversation is a conversation, not a disguised psychological examination.
> Voice and text are equal paths.
> The member should be able to interrupt Athena like a conversation, not operate her like an audio recording.
> Athena should yield when interrupted.
> The member does not perform engagement to remain eligible.
> Waiting is a legitimate designed state.
> People change, and Athena's understanding must be capable of changing with them.
> Important controls must not be buried because they permit departure or reduce engagement.
> Commercial conversion must never exploit emotional disclosure.
> Memory should feel useful, not invasive.
> Conversation never overrides authorization.
> Athena supports human connection; she does not compete with it.
> Sometimes Athena's best interaction is less Athena.

# 75. Relationship to E8

E7 establishes the interaction language. E8 applies it to the human relationship lifecycle: introduction experience; person revelation; individual consideration; decline; mutual interest; connection; messaging; first meeting; post-meeting reflection; developing relationship; Relationship Focus Mode; relationship support; endings; return to Athena; successful departure.

# 76. Relationship to Member Journey / UX Specification

After E1–E8 and the Experience Final Integration are canonical, the Member Journey / UX Specification converts E7 into explicit flows: screen sequence; navigation structure; onboarding steps; account creation placement; consent placement; payment placement; foundational conversation entry; voice/text controls; waiting surfaces; notification settings; Living Profile controls; account/security screens; error flows; recovery flows; accessibility behavior. E7 doctrine must not be prematurely converted into final screen layouts.

# 77. Canonical intent

Athena may contain enormous intelligence beneath the surface. The member should not have to operate that intelligence. They should simply be able to talk. Speak or type. Tell a story. Change their mind. Correct Athena. Leave. Come back. Pause. Listen. Ask. Meet someone. And eventually spend less time interacting with the technology because something more important has begun happening outside it.

The sophistication of Athena is measured partly by how little machinery the member needs to understand. The system can be complex. The interaction should feel human.

## 4. Questionnaire / dashboard / chatbot / engagement audit

- **Questionnaire behavior:** limited to the two necessary onboarding steps (§5-permitted); no personality tests, quizzes, matrices, or rating scales exist.
- **Dashboard behavior:** none — no statistics, charts, match counts, or completion percentages.
- **Generic chatbot behavior:** none — Athena runs under constitutional doctrine, topic mapping, and continuous understanding rather than a stock chat wrapper.
- **Engagement mechanics:** none — no streaks, daily prompts, badges, urgency, or return-nudging notifications.
- **Conventional dating-app patterns:** none — no swiping, browsing, ranking, catalogs, or match counters.
