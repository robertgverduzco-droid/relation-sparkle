# E6 — Motion, Curiosity & Revelation

**Status:** CANONICAL v1.0 · Canonized 2026-08-17
**Parent:** [Athena Experience Architecture](../athena-experience-architecture.md)
**Register:** [Decision & Implementation Register](./DECISION-REGISTER.md)

> Governing question: How should Athena's world move, reveal information,
> create anticipation, and signal meaning over time without becoming
> distracting, theatrical, addictive, or visually noisy?

---

# 1. Purpose

E6 defines the **temporal architecture** of the Athena experience. E1 established
Experience Philosophy, E2 the Emotional Journey, E3 Athena's Presence, E4 Voice &
Sonic Identity, E5 the Visual World. E6 establishes how those systems move
through time.

It governs: motion; transitions; listening, processing, speaking and waiting
states; progressive revelation; anticipation; connection-field behavior;
ceremony; visual pacing; timing; restrained surprise; motion accessibility.

E6 does **not** finalize: animation libraries; frame rates; easing curves; exact
durations; particle counts; physics engines; CSS implementation; final screen
transitions; haptic patterns; production motion tokens. Those belong to the later
Visual / Sonic / Interaction Design System and UX implementation.

# 2. Foundational motion principle

**Movement should mean something.** Athena never animates merely because motion
is available. Motion must support at least one of: presence, comprehension,
transition, attention, listening, processing, recognition, connection, emotional
pacing, hierarchy, ceremony. If it supports none of these, it should probably not
exist.

# 3. Motion is part of Athena's language

Members should come to understand, without instruction, when Athena is listening,
thinking, speaking, waiting, or has become quiet; when something meaningful has
changed; when a connection has formed; when a transition is occurring. Motion may
become semantic. It should not remain decorative.

# 4. Temporal character

Athena moves with calm, confidence, continuity, organic rhythm, restraint,
responsiveness. Her motion should rarely feel frantic, twitchy, playful for its
own sake, bouncy, game-like, aggressively fast, or mechanically repetitive.

# 5. Slow does not mean sluggish

Ambient motion may be slow. Interactive response must not be. The member must
never confuse calm with latency. Touch, navigation, interruption, input
acknowledgment and functional response remain immediate.

# 6. Never manufacture delay

Athena never creates artificial waiting to appear thoughtful. Results available
immediately are not held for theatrical effect. Processing states communicate
real processing; they do not simulate intelligence. A short meaningful pause in
conversation may be appropriate; artificial system latency is not.

# 7. Replace generic loading language

Where appropriate, Athena's genuine processing states are expressed through her
own motion language rather than a generic spinner — the direction already
recorded as Experience Decision **X-05**. The replacement must remain
understandable, accessible, technically honest and responsive. A generic progress
indicator may still be appropriate for functional system operations where
precision matters. Athena-native processing behavior must never obscure genuine
wait states.

# 8. Ambient motion

The environment may remain subtly alive without direct interaction: slow points,
gentle drift, subtle depth, low-energy spatial change. Ambient motion sits below
conscious attention. The member should feel life, not watch a screen saver.

# 9. The connection field

The slow-moving point field remains a promising experiential direction. Elements
may move independently, approach, diverge, briefly align, remain distant, form
temporary relationships, or form meaningful connections. The field is a symbolic
environment and must never become a literal visual database of members.

# 10. Independence before connection

Most elements exist independently. Connection does not erase individuality. The
field must not imply every point exists solely to find another.

# 11. Proximity

Proximity does not mean connection. People can meet, share characteristics, cross
paths and appear compatible without becoming meaningful partners. The field must
not imply proximity guarantees outcome.

# 12. Recognition

A meaningful moment may occur when the system visually recognizes a relationship
among elements — subtle change in trajectory, light, spacing, color or rhythm.
Recognition should feel discovered rather than triggered.

# 13. Connection

Connection is expressed elegantly: alignment, gentle illumination, shared motion,
color relationship, line formation, subtle stabilization. Never explosive lines,
hearts, fireworks, bright flashes, reward sounds or gamified celebration. Exact
treatment remains open.

# 14. Connection is not permanent fusion

Connected elements do not collapse into one object. The system preserves *two
individuals plus relationship*, never two individuals becoming one
indistinguishable object.

# 15. Motion must not predict destiny

No visual behavior may suggest a single destined pair, magnetic inevitability,
cosmic predetermination or soulmate certainty. The proposition is possibility and
recognition, not fate.

# 16. Listening state

When the member speaks, Athena has a recognizable listening state: subtle
responsiveness, soft expansion/contraction, slight reorganization, gentle rhythm
loosely linked to input presence. Avoid literal waveform dependence as identity.
The state communicates *I'm receiving you.*

# 17. Listening must not become surveillance theater

No biometric scanning imagery, face-analysis effects, voiceprint graphics,
emotional detection meters or diagnostic signals. Voice remains an interaction
channel under existing privacy doctrine (E4; Privacy & Security Architecture v1).

# 18. Processing state

Where genuine processing occurs, motion may become more organized: points
reorienting, subtle convergence, rebalancing, reduced ambient randomness. It
communicates *something is being considered*, not technological complexity.

# 19. Processing should be quiet

No frantic particle storms, spinning neural networks, scrolling code, rapidly
connecting nodes or intelligence spectacle. Sophistication through restraint.

# 20. Speaking state

Athena's visual presence may respond loosely to cadence, phrase rhythm, emphasis,
pause and energy. No literal equalizer or conventional waveform unless testing
proves it appropriate. Alive, not music playback software.

# 21. Text response state

Text motion remains coherent with voice mode: progressive appearance where
appropriate, reveal in meaningful units, slight transitions, space around
important thoughts. Avoid slow typewriter effects; reading is never deliberately
slowed for drama.

# 22. Reveal should follow meaning

Information need not appear simultaneously. Progressive revelation can convey
sequence, significance, context and emotional weight. **Reveal according to
meaning, not merely available screen space.**

# 23. Curiosity without withholding necessary information

Necessary information is never hidden to drive tapping. Prohibited dark patterns
include withholding price past reasonable disclosure, obscuring privacy terms,
hiding consequences of actions, and forcing multiple screens to reveal basic
facts. Curiosity belongs to discovery, not to informed-consent avoidance.

# 24. Progressive revelation of Athena

Members need not understand Athena completely at first launch. How she reasons,
remembers, introduces, supports and changes roles may be discovered gradually.
The architecture is not explained upfront.

# 25. Progressive revelation of the member

The experience does not demand complete self-disclosure immediately.
Understanding accumulates. Athena reveals herself gradually; the member reveals
themselves gradually. Trust develops through reciprocity of experience, not
reciprocal disclosure of private system internals.

# 26. Progressive revelation of another person

Introductions are the strongest use of progressive revelation: Athena's
attention, why this person is interesting, a meaningful characteristic, context,
photograph, further portrait information. Exact sequence belongs to E8. E6's
rule: **do not automatically lead with maximum visual information.**

# 27. Meaning may precede appearance

The member may first experience *why Athena became curious* before *what this
person looks like*. This does not suppress physical attraction; it prevents
appearance from prematurely erasing deeper context.

# 28. Reveal must respect privacy

Progressive revelation never authorizes private-data leakage. Athena reveals only
what counterpart-facing architecture permits. Mystery is not an excuse to hint at
confidential information; suggestive teasing of private material is prohibited.

# 29. Anticipation

Anticipation may surround genuine meaningful events — an introduction, mutual
interest, an important observation, entering Relationship Focus Mode. It emerges
because the event matters and is never manufactured around routine interactions.

# 30. Anticipation without anxiety

Timing must never create uncertainty about whether an action succeeded, a message
sent, payment processed, an account is secure, deletion occurred, or another
member responded. Functional certainty is immediate wherever possible.

# 31. Waiting state

Waiting is a legitimate product state (E2). It must not feel dead, broken, empty
or forgotten. The environment may remain quietly alive and Athena may
occasionally communicate relevant status. No meaningless activity is invented to
fill the wait.

# 32. No fake progress

No fake matchmaking progress bars, arbitrary percentages, "83% closer to your
match", false search animations, fake candidate counts or synthetic progress
indicators. If progress cannot be measured, it is not visualized.

# 33. Transition philosophy

Transitions connect states rather than decorate them: onboarding → Athena;
foundational conversation → waiting; waiting → introduction; introduction →
connection; connection → Focus Mode. Transitions support orientation.

# 34. Spatial continuity

Where useful, screens feel spatially connected; elements transform rather than
disappear and reappear arbitrarily. Athena's world should feel continuous.

# 35. Navigation motion

Navigation is fast and understandable. Avoid dramatic page slides, excessive
parallax, long fades and cinematic transitions for routine navigation. Routine
movement remains routine.

# 36. Ceremony

Ceremony is a temporary change in pacing, movement, space, light, typography and
possibly sound that acknowledges meaningful transition. Ceremony should be rare.

# 37. First arrival ceremony

First arrival may receive the strongest environmental introduction: depth,
ambient connection field, sonic signature, Athena's voice, restrained visual
organization. The member should feel arrival, not marketing spectacle.

# 38. Foundational conversation completion

Completion may receive subtle acknowledgment — visual settling, slight field
organization, Athena's closing language, space. No completion badges, scores,
"100% profile complete", or confetti. The message is *Athena now knows enough to
begin.*

# 39. Introduction ceremony

The environment may quiet, motion may slow, Athena may come forward briefly, and
the person is revealed progressively. The member should sense Athena chose to
bring this forward for a reason.

# 40. Mutual connection ceremony

A restrained shift only. No fireworks, exploding hearts or slot-machine reward
design. Two people have independently chosen curiosity; that is enough.

# 41. Relationship Focus Mode ceremony

The environment may simplify, matching-oriented movement decrease,
connection-oriented behavior stabilize, and Athena's presence subtly recede. The
product makes room for the relationship.

# 42. Successful departure ceremony

Leaving matchmaking because a relationship succeeded may deserve one of the most
elegant transitions in Athena. The product must not look disappointed. The member
should feel completion, possibility, dignity, quiet celebration — communicated
through space rather than Athena's centrality.

# 43. Error motion

Errors require clarity. No aggressive shaking, flashing, dramatic red animation
or failure spectacle. Error motion guides attention; it does not punish.

# 44. Safety motion

Safety states may require stronger signals. Clarity outranks elegance. Motion
must not heighten panic unnecessarily. Trust & Safety doctrine remains
controlling.

# 45. Motion and emotional context

Vulnerability → movement decreases. Ordinary conversation → subtle presence. Joy
→ slightly increased energy may be appropriate. Seriousness → stable, restrained
movement. Ceremony → temporary intentional change. Motion supports the
conversation; it never competes with it.

# 46. Motion and Athena's receding presence

As the member moves from Athena-centered toward human-relationship-centered, the
environment becomes less active around Athena, less animated, more stable, more
human-content-focused. Motion communicates Athena stepping back.

# 47. No engagement animation

Prohibited: swipe-card momentum, endless bouncing cues, persistent pulsing CTAs,
reward loops, celebration after ordinary engagement, animated unread pressure.
Motion never becomes behavioral coercion.

# 48. Microinteractions

Feedback for button acknowledgment, toggle state, save confirmation, correction
submitted, audio mode changed should be quick, clear, consistent and restrained,
and must not compete with Athena's larger motion language.

# 49. Haptics

Haptics may eventually support confirmation, meaningful connection and critical
warning. They must be sparse and never become reward conditioning. Exact behavior
remains unresolved.

# 50. Reduced motion

Members must be able to use Athena comfortably with reduced-motion preferences.
Where movement carries meaning, equivalents are required. Reduced motion must not
produce a lesser product. Platform accessibility preferences are respected where
technically appropriate.

# 51. Motion sensitivity

Avoid motion likely to create dizziness, nausea, visual fatigue or sensory
overload. Large-scale parallax, rapid zoom, flashing and aggressive spatial
movement are avoided by default.

# 52. Performance

A beautiful transition that stutters feels less premium than no transition.
Motion must respect device capability, battery, network state, browser
limitations, accessibility and PWA constraints. Graceful degradation is required.

# 53. Loading under poor conditions

Where latency is substantial, the member receives honest status. Athena-native
motion may communicate that work continues; beyond reasonable expectation,
language explains it. Atmospheric animation must never hide technical failure.

# 54. Offline / interrupted state

Visual behavior distinguishes Athena processing, network unavailable, request
failed, and service temporarily unavailable. One generic animated state must not
obscure different operational realities.

# 55. Temporal consistency

Motion uses a coherent timing language. Routine actions feel related; Athena
states feel related; ceremonies may be slower; functional actions quicker. The
system should eventually develop reusable motion tokens. Exact timings belong
downstream.

# 56. Rhythm

The experience has rhythm: movement → stillness → reveal → space → response.
Rhythm creates emphasis; continuous movement destroys it.

# 57. Stillness is a motion state

Athena may become nearly still during important reflection, vulnerable
disclosure, major realization, or relationship transition. Stillness signals
attention.

# 58. Rare surprise

Occasional visual surprise may create delight. It must remain coherent with
Athena, contextually meaningful, and rare enough to retain impact. No rotating
collection of novelty animations.

# 59. Motion should age well

Avoid trend-heavy motion. Athena's motion language aspires to be timeless,
organic, disciplined, subtle and responsive.

# 60. The E6 motion test

1. What does this movement mean?
2. Would the experience lose meaning if the motion disappeared?
3. Is the member informed or merely entertained?
4. Does the timing respect attention?
5. Is curiosity supported without withholding necessary information?
6. Does it preserve calm?
7. Is it honest about system state?
8. Is it accessible?
9. Could it become repetitive or annoying after hundreds of uses?
10. Does it feel like Athena?
11. Does it reinforce the connection-field visual language?
12. Does it avoid generic AI and dating-app animation?
13. Is ceremony proportional to significance?
14. Does Athena become quieter when human connection becomes more important?
15. Does the movement support humanity rather than display technology?

# 61. Permanent E6 principles

> Movement should mean something.
> Calm must never be confused with latency.
> Never manufacture delay to simulate intelligence.
> Athena's motion should communicate state without displaying technological spectacle.
> Connection does not erase individuality.
> Proximity is not destiny.
> Reveal according to meaning, not merely available screen space.
> Curiosity is not permission to hide necessary information.
> Meaning may precede appearance.
> Waiting should feel intentional without fake progress.
> Ceremony acknowledges change; spectacle demands attention.
> Stillness is a motion state.
> Athena-native processing should replace generic spinners where appropriate without obscuring real latency or error.
> Motion must become quieter as Athena's role becomes less central.
> Movement supports humanity; it does not advertise technology.

# 62. Relationship to E7

E7 will establish Member Interaction & Conversation Experience — navigation,
onboarding sequence, member input, voice/text controls, foundational conversation
UX, interruptions, Living Profile interaction, correction/removal, notification
experience, waiting experience, account controls, payment placement,
accessibility behavior. E6 provides the temporal language E7 may use.

# 63. Relationship to E8

E8 will apply E6 to introductions, progressive person reveal, mutual connection,
messaging, meetings, reflections, Relationship Focus Mode, endings and return.

# 64. Relationship to the Design System

After E1–E8, the Visual / Sonic / Interaction Design System converts E6 into
implementation specifications: motion durations, easing, transition families,
particle physics, reduced-motion behavior, haptic patterns, loading-state
behavior, animation performance budgets, motion tokens, and choreography between
sound and motion. None of these are implemented during E6 canonicalization.

# 65. Canonical intent

Athena's world should never feel static, and never restless. Movement should
create the sense that something intelligent is present beneath the interface.
Sometimes that intelligence listens, organizes, reveals, connects, waits, or
becomes completely still. And when Athena has helped two people find something
meaningful in one another, the most sophisticated motion choice may be for Athena
herself to move quietly out of the center — and leave two human beings with room
to move toward each other.

# Canonical review record (2026-08-17)

**Prerequisite check.** E1–E5 confirmed CANONICAL v1.0 prior to this review.

**Doctrinal comparison.** E6 was compared against the Constitution (L1–L7),
Athena University curriculum, Product Architecture, Experience Architecture
(E1–E5), Privacy & Security Architecture v1, Trust & Safety doctrine, and
accessibility requirements. **No doctrinal conflict was found.** E6 §17 and §28
restate, and do not extend, existing privacy boundaries; §44 defers to Trust &
Safety; §32 and §15 reinforce the existing prohibitions on scoring, ranking and
deterministic matching; §46 extends E2's receding-presence principle into time.
No duplicated authority: E5 governs static visual identity, E6 governs its
behavior over time, E7/E8 govern interaction and relationship-specific
expression.

**Reconciliation with X-05.** E6 §7 is the doctrinal home of X-05. X-05 remains
open and is now scoped by E6: Athena-native processing language replaces generic
indicators only where it remains understandable, accessible, honest about latency
and error, and free of artificial delay; conventional progress indicators remain
permitted for functional system operations where precision matters.

**Current runtime motion inventory (observed, unchanged).**

| Behavior | Location | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Landing connection/point field, continuous `requestAnimationFrame` canvas animation | `src/components/landing-background.tsx` | Provisional atmospheric implementation; no `prefers-reduced-motion` path, no performance/battery budget → X-16, X-19 |
| Global route fade (`ri-fade`, 1200 ms) | `src/styles.css` | Single undifferentiated entrance animation; no state-aware transition family → X-20 |
| Thinking indicator: pulsing dot (`animate-pulse`) | `src/routes/_authenticated/athena.tsx` | Generic placeholder rather than Athena-native processing language → X-05, X-17 |
| Listening/microphone active state (`animate-pulse` on the record control) | `src/routes/_authenticated/athena.tsx` | Generic pulse; not yet a designed listening state → X-17 |
| Onboarding step/progress bar | `src/routes/_authenticated/onboarding.tsx` | Permitted: measures the member's own flow, never a person (F-13). Must not migrate to comparison surfaces → X-13 |
| shadcn primitive transitions (dialog, sheet, tooltip, accordion, etc.) | `src/components/ui/*` | Functional microinteractions; consistent with §48. To be unified under motion tokens later → X-20 |
| Streaming text response | `src/routes/_authenticated/athena.tsx` | Progressive reveal reflects genuine token streaming, not theatrical typewriter pacing. Consistent with §21 |

**Risk findings.** No fake progress, no engagement animation, no reward loops, no
swipe momentum, no celebration animation and no dating-app motion patterns exist
in current runtime. Two real gaps: (1) no reduced-motion support anywhere,
notably for the continuously animating connection field (§50, §51) → X-16;
(2) a single generic thinking/loading vocabulary that cannot distinguish
processing from network failure or service unavailability (§53, §54) → X-18.

**Unresolved production specifications.** Connection-field behavior, particle
behavior, reveal choreography, ceremony, haptics, timings and motion tokens
remain unresolved and are owned by the later Design System phase (X-19, X-20,
X-21).

**Changes made in this pass.** Documentation only. No animation library, CSS,
transition, loader, particle system, component or UI behavior was modified.
