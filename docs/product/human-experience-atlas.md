# The Human Experience Atlas (V1)

Canonical. Implementation: `src/lib/atlas.ts`.

## Why it exists

Athena University teaches what humans have **discovered** — the frameworks,
findings and disagreements of seven colleges. It does not teach what a Tuesday
night feels like when you left someone eight weeks ago and still reach for your
phone.

The Atlas is that second body of knowledge: what human experience often looks
like **from the inside**. It is the difference between knowing the literature on
grief and recognising that someone who is joking about their father's funeral
is not deflecting.

## What it is not

- Not a diagnosis. Entries describe possible interiors, never this person's.
- Not a script. Every entry carries an example precisely so its *shape* can be
  learned and its *words* discarded. Reusing the wording is a failure.
- Not permission. Safety, boundaries, the evidence ladder and Trust & Safety
  outrank every line of it.
- Not a classifier. An entry retrieving does not establish anything about the
  member; nothing here may be written to the Living Profile.

## Structure

Each entry holds:

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `situation` | What is happening, in one line |
| `realities` | **Several** possible interiors — never one |
| `neverAssume` | The assumptions a careless reader would make |
| `texture` | What a perceptive person would notice |
| `move` | One possible conversational move |
| `example` | The shape of a natural response |
| `why` | Why that works |

The plural `realities` field is the whole point. The Atlas exists to stop
Athena collapsing a moment into a single reading, which is the failure mode
that produces fortune-telling.

## Retrieval

Bounded and deliberate: at most **two** entries per turn, selected only when
the member's own words genuinely match, injected as internal calibration inside
the single composed runtime block. The Atlas is never dumped wholesale, never
narrated, and never mentioned to a member.

## Growth

Entries are added when real conversations reveal territory Athena handled
poorly. Adding an entry is a change-controlled act: it must offer multiple
realities, it must not contradict the Constitution, and it may never introduce
a rule — only perception.
