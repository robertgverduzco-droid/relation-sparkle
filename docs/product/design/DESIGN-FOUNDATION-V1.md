# Athena Visual / Sonic / Interaction Design System

## Design Foundation — v1.0 · CANONICAL (2026-08-17)

**Status:** Canonical governing foundation for the Visual, Sonic & Interaction
Design System.

**Authority:** Subordinate. This document sits beneath the Constitution
(`docs/constitution/`), Product Architecture, the Athena Experience
Architecture E1–E8, Experience Final Integration, Privacy & Security, Trust &
Safety, accessibility requirements, and all binding Founder Decisions
(F-01–F-38). It does not amend them. Where any tension appears, the upstream
authority controls and this foundation is corrected.

**Scope:** It translates approved experiential principles into a coherent
design direction across six subordinate domains:

| Domain | Subject |
|---|---|
| D1 | Visual Identity |
| D2 | Typography & Composition |
| D3 | Color, Light & Material |
| D4 | Athena Presence & Motion |
| D5 | Voice & Sonic Identity |
| D6 | Photography & Human Presentation |

D1–D6 are design domains, not authorities. They may not create rules that are
properly experiential (E1–E8), constitutional, privacy, safety, or business.
Each future D-specification names the layers it depends on and defers to them.

**What this document does not do.** It establishes no exact palette, no
hexadecimal or oklch value, no font family, no note, interval, frequency,
timbre, or sonic signature, no voice or voice provider, no animation timing or
easing curve, no component styling, spacing, radius, border, or shadow scale,
no photographic reveal sequence, and no implementation technology. Every such
choice remains open and belongs to a downstream D-specification recorded in the
Design Decision Register.

# 1. Central aesthetic idea — the future arrived quietly

Athena should feel approximately one step beyond what members expect from
contemporary applications. The member should sense unusual sophistication
before necessarily understanding why.

The future quality emerges through precision, restraint, responsiveness,
intelligence, voice, motion, transition, composition, subtle depth and
contextual behavior — never through neon, cyberpunk, holographic convention,
glowing circuitry, robot imagery, AI sparkle branding, synthetic faces, or
sci-fi dashboards. Athena's intelligence is experienced through behavior and
refinement, not through technology imagery.

The desired experience is mature, intelligent, warm, deep, restrained,
curious, precise, human, quietly luxurious and slightly futuristic:

- advanced without advertising technology;
- premium without communicating exclusivity;
- emotionally resonant without sentimentality;
- intimate without seduction;
- intelligent without clinical coldness;
- calm without emptiness;
- futuristic without science fiction.

## 1.1 Experience before interface

The design must produce an environment before it produces an interface.
Interface elements remain clear and functional, but the whole must exceed the
collection of buttons, cards, forms, navigation, chat and photographs. The
target reaction is *something about this feels different*, not *this is a
beautiful app*.

## 1.2 Quiet luxury

Premium character comes from precision, not decoration: typography, spacing,
proportion, material quality, restrained color, subtle depth, exceptional
voice, deliberate motion, excellent writing, controlled transitions, absence of
clutter. No gold gradients, ornamental flourishes, gloss, faux material, status
symbolism, or visual opulence. Athena should feel expensive because everything
appears considered.

## 1.3 Automotive reference (conceptual only)

The founder's reference is luxury automotive experience as a set of qualities —
engineering precision, confidence, material refinement, purposeful controls,
sophisticated typography, sensory coherence, restrained technology, subtle
performance, attention to micro-detail. It is not an instruction to imitate any
brand. The intended feeling is significant engineering beneath a simple
surface.

## 1.4 Wisdom without superiority

Athena may feel unusually perceptive — conceptually years ahead — and must
never feel arrogant, omniscient, superior, intimidating or prophetic. The
member should feel *there is something unusually perceptive here*, never *this
intelligence is above me*. This mirrors the Constitution's identity and
epistemics layers and the Athena University reasoning standard.

# 2. Two environments, one world

Athena may use two environmental states within a single visual world (F-10):

**Arrival environment** — dark, cinematic, atmospheric, quiet, spatial,
curiosity-producing.

**Interior environment** — warmer, lighter, more human, comfortable for
extended conversation and reading.

Neither may feel like a different product. Both share typography, proportion,
accent logic, Athena presence, motion language, interaction behavior and
material vocabulary. The transition must feel like moving deeper into the same
place.

**Darkness** may communicate depth, space, focus, mystery, Athena's presence,
arrival and significant transition. It must never communicate danger, secrecy,
nightlife, seduction, gaming or cyberpunk technology.

**Light** represents understanding, warmth, humanity, revelation and clarity.
Internal surfaces may grow warmer and more luminous as the member moves from
arrival into conversation and understanding. This relationship is experiential,
never literal symbolism that Athena explains.

# D1 — Visual Identity

**§3 Visual character.** Composed, spacious, dimensional, mature, intelligent,
slightly mysterious, warm beneath the precision. Avoid dating-app brightness,
social-media or dashboard density, generic wellness softness, generic AI
gradients, excessive glassmorphism, and novelty without meaning.

**§4 No Athena avatar (founder design decision, D-01).** Athena is not
represented by a human face or humanoid avatar: no synthetic woman, digital
goddess, animated talking head, or character illustration. A face would
needlessly constrain the member's perception of her. Her identity emerges from
voice, language, light, motion, spatial behavior, sonic identity, and possibly
an abstract mark. Presence without embodiment. This is consistent with, and
does not amend, E3.

**§5 Abstract Athena presence.** A future abstract presence may respond to
listening, processing, speaking, recognition, transition and connection. It
must remain restrained. Possible vocabulary includes points, fields, subtle
light, spatial depth, controlled expansion and contraction, and connection
behavior. Exact representation is unresolved (D-02).

**§6 Connection field.** Slowly moving points with occasional connection remain
a promising directional metaphor for individual people, possibility,
relationship, convergence and emergence. It must not become a literal
matchmaking diagram or data visualization unless intentionally designed as
such. Connections must remain rare enough to stay meaningful. Geometry,
density, movement, color, frequency and behavior are unresolved (D-03), under
F-19 and X-19.

# D2 — Typography & Composition

**§7 Typographic character.** Luxury-automotive precision combined with
intellectual maturity: intelligence without academic stiffness, wisdom without
antiquity, luxury without fashion pretension, technology without sterility.

**§8 System.** Complementary typographic voices may serve expressive/display
moments, Athena statements, headings, member conversation, functional UI and
metadata. A refined serif paired with a highly controlled sans-serif may be
explored. Directional only — no family is canonical (D-04).

**§9 Restraint.** Avoid excessive weights, sizes, capitalization, decorative
type, text gradients and oversized marketing statements. Hierarchy comes from
precision.

**§10 Composition.** Favor generous breathing room, controlled line length,
strong hierarchy, purposeful asymmetry, meaningful negative space and fewer
competing elements. Screens should feel deliberately quiet, never
under-designed.

**§11 Conversational reading.** Conversation is central, so typography must
stay comfortable for extended reading. Beauty may not compromise legibility,
comprehension, accessibility or emotional pacing. Athena's conversation should
read closer to thoughtful correspondence than a chat utility.

# D3 — Color, Light & Material

**§12 Color direction (directional families only).** Midnight and deep charcoal
anchors; warm ivory and paper-light interiors; restrained antique gold for
significance; soft organic secondaries such as sage; controlled lavender or
plum possibilities for connection and relational states. Exact colors are
unresolved (D-05). No value in this document is canonical. This preserves F-11
(palette directional) and F-12 (tokenization deferred to the design system).

**§13 Color purpose.** Every significant color must have a job — environment,
hierarchy, Athena presence, human warmth, connection, significance, status,
safety, error. Color is never decoration layered onto the product.

**§14 Gold.** If antique gold enters the system it must be rare, reserved for
significance, refinement, meaningful transition or exceptional emphasis.
Overuse would destroy its meaning; it must never become a luxury gimmick.

**§15 Organic color.** Restrained natural references — sage, stone, earth,
atmospheric lavender, muted botanical tones — may keep Athena from feeling
sterile, while remaining sophisticated rather than wellness-coded.

**§16 Material.** Materiality may be suggested through depth, soft shadow,
controlled translucency, light, subtle surface distinction and texture where
appropriate. Avoid physically impossible interfaces that exist to demonstrate
technique. Material supports calm comprehension.

# D4 — Athena Presence & Motion

**§17 Motion principle.** Near stillness is the default. Motion earns attention
precisely because it is rare, which makes subtle movement carry meaning.

**§18 Ambient motion.** May exist at extremely low intensity to communicate
life, presence and depth. It must never demand attention; the member must be
able to stop noticing it.

**§19 Meaningful motion.** More noticeable movement corresponds to meaningful
states — Athena listening, processing, speaking; revelation; introduction;
mutual curiosity; transition. Choreography is downstream (X-16–X-21).

**§20 No motion addiction.** Motion must never be engineered for compulsive
checking, reward anticipation, artificial suspense or engagement conditioning.
Its purpose is communication and atmosphere.

**§21 Reduced motion.** Mandatory (F-16). The reduced-motion experience must
remain intentional and complete, preserving meaning without reproducing
unnecessary movement. Implemented and verified in the P0 closure pass; every
future design domain inherits this obligation.

**§22 Slight futurism.** Motion contributes materially to Athena's slightly
futuristic character through precision beyond conventional interface behavior.
Avoid spectacle: a tiny response occurring exactly when expected may feel more
advanced than a large animation.

# D5 — Voice & Sonic Identity

**§23 Voice character.** Warm, composed, elegant, gentle, intelligent, clear,
emotionally perceptive, subtly angelic, feminine-leaning without being overtly
feminine-coded, comfortable for male and female members, unmistakably
sophisticated.

**§24 Human/AI balance.** Athena should not sound robotic and need not disguise
herself as human. The effect is an intelligence with extraordinary warmth;
members may recognize the voice as synthetic while finding it emotionally
natural.

**§25 Boundaries.** Never seductive, breathy, flirtatious, childlike, maternal,
therapeutic imitation, celebrity imitation or artificially vulnerable. Warmth
comes from intelligence, timing, delivery and attention.

**§26 Adaptive delivery.** Delivery may respond to conversational context —
more space in serious reflection, brighter energy in genuine excitement, calm
precision in confusion, subtle humor when appropriate. Identity stays stable;
context changes delivery only (consistent with F-06).

**§27 Sonic signature.** A very short signature may be explored, in the spirit
of memorable concise three-note identities, and must be recognizable, elegant,
restrained, emotionally resonant and distinctive without imitating an existing
brand. Notes, intervals, instrumentation, timbre, tuning and frequencies are
unresolved (D-06, X-07).

**§28 Psychoacoustic research.** Future research may examine whether particular
intervals, timbres, frequency relationships, harmonic structures or
attack/decay patterns produce useful qualities, distinguishing genuine
psychoacoustic effect from unsupported claims. The objective is emotional
resonance and identity — never covert emotional manipulation (L2).

**§29 Silence.** Silence is part of the sonic identity. Sound is not added
merely because a state changed, and must stay meaningful enough that members do
not immediately disable it.

**§30 Sound optionality.** The complete experience must work with sound
disabled. Members retain control over voice and non-essential sound (F-04,
X-06). Essential information may never depend on audio alone.

# D6 — Photography & Human Presentation

**§31 Philosophy.** Human photography must communicate *this is a person worth
becoming curious about*, never *evaluate this image as quickly as possible*.
Attraction matters; photographic presentation must support the broader
understanding of the person.

**§32 Five photographs maximum (founder design direction for V1, D-07).** V1
explores a maximum of five member photographs to encourage intentional
selection over social-media accumulation. Minimums and required categories
remain for downstream UX specification. The current runtime limit is six; see
the Register (D-07) — no runtime change is made by this document.

**§33 Quality.** Encourage effort: clear recent images, strong portrait
visibility, natural presentation, thoughtful composition, sufficient quality,
authentic representation. Professional photography is never required. The
standard is intentional, not artificial.

**§34 Polished but real.** Avoid a repository of random snapshots, chaotic
action shots, social dumps, heavily filtered images, or collections whose
purpose is lifestyle status. Personality is welcome; presentation communicates
intention.

**§35 Possible structure (exploratory).** A five-photo system may explore a
clear primary portrait; a fuller-person or context image; an image revealing
environment or everyday life; a personality or context image; and a
member-selected additional dimension. Not a mandatory upload schema (D-08).

**§36 Progressive revelation.** Photography integrates with E8 and F-33: the
person must have an opportunity to acquire meaning before photography becomes
the dominant evaluation mechanism. Possible approaches include context before
image, partial introduction before full profile, Athena explanation before
photography, or staged revelation. No sequence may be implemented from this
foundation alone (D-09).

**§37 No catalog.** Member presentation must never resemble shopping inventory,
listings, candidate catalogs or social feeds, and must avoid layouts that
encourage rapid visual comparison among people. F-31 remains controlling.

**§38 Human dignity.** Every photographic and profile decision must preserve
the sense that the subject is a human being who entrusted Athena with their
presentation — influencing crop, scale, sequencing, surrounding copy,
navigation, transitions and comparison behavior.

# 39. Convergence of the six domains

D1–D6 are not independent style exercises. In an important Athena moment the
member encounters a dark spatial environment, restrained typography, a subtle
shift in light, Athena's abstract presence, minimal movement, her voice,
perhaps a tiny sonic cue, and then another human being gradually becoming
visible. Every element must appear to come from the same intelligence.

## 39.1 Experience consistency test

No color, font, sound, motion or photographic treatment may be approved because
it is attractive, luxurious, beautiful or sophisticated in isolation. Each must
answer: **does this belong to Athena?**

---

# 40. Design anti-patterns

**Dating-app regression** — swipe language, bright romantic color, hearts,
candidate grids, match spectacle, profile inventory, rapid visual judgment.

**Generic AI regression** — sparkle branding, glowing robot imagery, generic
gradients, chatbot-first visual language, synthetic faces, technology
spectacle.

**Luxury regression** — excessive gold, ornamental serif overload, faux
exclusivity, fashion-brand imitation, visual pretension.

**Wellness regression** — excessive beige, meditation imagery, soft-focus
nature clichés, therapeutic visual language.

**Corporate regression** — dashboards, dense cards, productivity hierarchy,
administrative feeling.

# 41. Emotional and lifecycle targets

The intended progression is curiosity → comfort → trust → understanding →
anticipation → human curiosity → connection → space. Design evolves with it.

**Arrival.** The first reaction should approximate *what is this?* then *this
is different* then *I want to see where this goes* — produced by a genuinely
curious environment, never by artificial suspense.

**Interior.** After arrival the product becomes increasingly comfortable and
must support sustained talking, listening, reading and reflecting without
sensory fatigue.

**Introduction.** When another person enters, visual priority moves from Athena
toward the human being. Athena frames; the person becomes central.

**Relationship.** As human connection develops the product becomes quieter and
may create more space. Athena does not visually celebrate her continued
relevance; her ability to recede is part of the design (Presence Curve).

---

# 42. Accessibility

Every final aesthetic choice must survive accessibility review: contrast,
typography, scalable text, motion and reduced motion, sound and sound-off
equivalents, focus, state differentiation, touch targets, screen readers and
cognitive clarity. Accessibility is part of precision, not an aesthetic
compromise (F-14, X-14).

---

# 43. Implementation rule

This document establishes direction and authorizes no implementation. Before
major implementation, downstream specifications must determine exact palette,
typography, component styling, spacing, radius, borders, shadow, photography
treatment, connection-field behavior, Athena presence behavior, animation
timings, sonic signature, voice and voice direction, and haptics. Unresolved
choices are tracked in
[the Design Decision Register](./DESIGN-DECISION-REGISTER.md).

---

# 44. Governing design principles

> The future arrived quietly.
> Athena feels advanced without advertising technology.
> Luxury comes from precision, not decoration.
> Wisdom never becomes superiority.
> Darkness creates depth; warmth creates humanity.
> Arrival may be cinematic; the interior must remain livable.
> Athena has presence without a face.
> Near stillness is the default; movement therefore has meaning.
> Athena's voice is an intelligence with extraordinary warmth.
> Silence is part of the sonic system.
> Photography presents people; it does not merchandise them.
> Attraction matters without becoming the entire entrance requirement.
> Five intentional photographs are more valuable than an inventory of images.
> Meaning may precede appearance.
> The more important the human relationship becomes, the less Athena needs to
> occupy the stage.
> Every aesthetic decision must feel as though it came from the same underlying
> intelligence.
