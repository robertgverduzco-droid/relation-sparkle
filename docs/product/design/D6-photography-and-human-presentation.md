# D6 — Photography & Human Presentation

## v1.0 · CANONICAL (2026-08-17)

**Status:** Canonical. Sixth and final domain specification beneath the
[Design Foundation](./DESIGN-FOUNDATION-V1.md), inheriting from
[D1 — Visual Identity](./D1-visual-identity.md),
[D2 — Typography & Composition](./D2-typography-and-composition.md),

**Authority:** Subordinate. D6 sits beneath the Constitution

Experience Architecture E1–E8, Experience
[Final Integration](../experience/FINAL-INTEGRATION.md), the Design Foundation,
D1–D5, and all binding Founder Decisions (F-01–F-38, D-01, D-07). It amends
none of them.

**Scope:** Member photography, photo quantity and quality, human presentation,
photographic hierarchy, introduction imagery, progressive revelation, visual
attraction, dignity, authenticity, photo controls, and the relationship between
understanding and appearance.

**Explicitly out of scope:** D6 defines no final page layout, aspect ratio,
minimum photo count, moderation technology, caption policy, group/child photo
policy, synthetic-photo policy, filter threshold, alt-text implementation or
revelation choreography. Those remain open in the
[Design Decision Register](./DESIGN-DECISION-REGISTER.md).

**Governing question.** How should Athena reveal and visually present another
human being so physical attraction remains real and important, while the person
is experienced as a human being rather than inventory?

---

## 1. Core principle

> Athena presents a person. She does not display inventory.

Photography helps one human encounter another. It must never create the
behavioral rhythm of shopping through people.

## 2. Physical attraction is real

Athena does not pretend appearance is irrelevant. Attraction may matter
substantially, and members must be able to see another person clearly enough to
experience genuine attraction or its absence. Athena's distinction is **sequence
and context, not denial of appearance**.

## 3. Understanding precedes comparison

Photography enters an introduction as part of understanding a particular person.
It never creates a catalog for rapid comparison.

## 4. Single-person attention (F-31 binding)

One human being commands attention. Forbidden: candidate photo grids, horizontal
people carousels, swipe stacks, ranked alternatives, "similar people",
side-by-side comparison. The member encounters someone, not a selection screen.

## 5. Five-photo maximum (D-07 binding)

A member may have at most five photographs in V1. Five is a ceiling, not a
required minimum. The former runtime allowance of six was repaired on
2026-08-19; five is now enforced in the uploader, the counterpart read path and
the database.

**Why five.** Enough range to convey face, physical presence, personality, life
context and variation, without becoming a social-media gallery. Each image
should add information.

## 6. Intentionality and polished humanity

The desired behavior is: *I should put some thought into how I present myself
here.* Athena encourages care without demanding professional photography. The
quality target is **polished humanity** — professionally taken, or carefully
selected, naturally lit, well composed and authentic personal photographs.
Polished does not mean an idealized advertising version of the member.

## 7. Primary photograph and face visibility

A member designates or receives a clearly governed primary photograph that makes
them recognizable, unobstructed, sufficiently visible and naturally presented.
At least one photograph clearly shows the face. Avoid a primary presentation
dominated by sunglasses, masks, extreme distance, heavy obstruction, another
person or aggressive filtering. Exact requirements and moderation policy are
downstream (D-41).

## 8. Fuller human context

The set may include more than portraits: clear portrait, fuller-body/context
image, activity or interest, travel/environment, personally meaningful setting,
relaxed candid. Directional categories, not a checklist.

**Action photographs** are allowed but must not dominate the set. Athena
encourages a somewhat more intentional and polished presentation than
conventional dating apps.

**Group photographs** may create ambiguity; if allowed, the member must remain
immediately identifiable. Nobody should have to work out which person owns the
profile. **Children and non-members** raise privacy and presentation questions;
final policy is downstream under Privacy & Security and Trust & Safety (D-42).

## 9. Authenticity

Photography represents the member reasonably as they currently are. Future
policy addresses materially outdated photos, deceptive editing, face alteration,
synthetic imagery and AI-generated representation. Athena protects authentic
presentation without becoming appearance police.

**Filters.** Ordinary photographic correction may be acceptable; material
alteration of physical appearance is discouraged or governed.

**AI-generated member photography.** V1 strongly favors actual photographs of
the member. Any future synthetic-photo policy requires explicit governance
(D-43).

## 10. Material treatment

**Natural color.** Member photography retains natural skin tone and
environmental color. The design system may frame images; it must not impose a
brand grade that makes every human look like part of the same advertisement
(inherits D3 §human photography ungraded).

**Cropping.** Preserve dignity and useful context. Avoid automated crops that
cut faces awkwardly, sexualize bodies, remove meaningful context or mislead.

**Shape and scale.** Aspect ratios are downstream. Avoid treatments resembling
product cards; human photography uses larger, calmer presentation with
surrounding space, large enough to experience the person. Avoid tiny thumbnails
surrounded by metadata.

**No rapid consumption.** No rapid tapping through images, swipe-through
evaluation, thumbnail scanning or comparison behavior. Members may explore
photographs naturally; tempo remains deliberate.

## 11. Progressive revelation (F-33 binding)

> Meaning precedes full appearance.

This does not require hiding photographs for an arbitrary time. It requires the
introduction to establish enough human context that appearance is not the first
and only information.

**No artificial delay.** Revelation must never become a countdown, timed lock,
engagement mechanic, suspense game or mandatory waiting period. Athena never
manipulates curiosity by withholding photographs.

**V1 directional sequence.**
1. **Athena frames the person** — briefly, why this particular person became
   worth considering. No numerical compatibility, no counterpart reasoning, no
   excessive biography.
2. **Human context emerges** — a small amount of meaningful information: first
   name, appropriate location context, selected human details, a short framing
   statement.
3. **Photography becomes available** — the primary photograph joins the
   introduction.
4. **The member chooses whether to know more** — additional permitted context
   and photographs become naturally available.

Exact screen choreography is resolved in
[D-44 / F-33 Progressive Visual Revelation](./D-44-progressive-visual-revelation.md). The sequence should be fast
enough to feel honest; its purpose is only to prevent the encounter beginning
and ending with a photograph.

## 12. Athena does not pre-sell the person

Revelation must not emotionally prime the member into liking someone before the
photograph. Framing is specific, measured, curious and honest. Forbidden
language: "perfect for you", "incredible match", "soulmate", "exactly what you
need". Certainty language remains governed by X-32; **visual presentation must
not amplify certainty beyond Athena's actual reasoning.** No visual aura of
destiny.

## 13. The reveal and the Presence Curve

The primary photograph arrives with dignity. Forbidden: dramatic
blur-to-focus suspense, card flip, curtain reveal, slot-machine movement, zoom
explosion, match sound, countdown. A restrained transition suffices.

When the photograph becomes visible, **Athena visually recedes** — implementing
the Presence Curve (D4 §Athena recedes). The member looks at the person, not the
interface.

## 14. Information around photography

Never surround the image with scores, percentages, badges, ranking labels,
popularity indicators or activity metrics. The human being is the focal point.

**Age** may be displayed as factual information where product and legal
requirements apply — never treated visually as ranking metadata.

## 15. Location and metadata privacy

Location remains governed by metro-level privacy architecture. Photography must
not expose more precise location through metadata, captions or automatic
location labels. **Existing canvas re-encoding that removes EXIF/GPS before
upload must remain; visual redesign must not regress it.**

**Visual privacy.** Member photography is sensitive; future implementation
should consider reasonable visual-privacy treatments consistent with platform
capabilities. D6 promises no technically impossible screenshot prevention.

**Counterpart privacy (F-37).** Photography creates no access to counterpart
Living Profile, private Athena reasoning, sensitive inferences, partner
perception or private reflections. The introduction boundary remains sealed.

## 16. Member control over photographs

Members understand which photos are active, which is primary, and how to replace
or remove them; management is simple. Captions, if used, add meaningful context
rather than social-media captioning (downstream). Members likely control photo
order subject to primary-photo requirements; **Athena never silently reorders
people by predicted attractiveness.**

**Deletion** follows Athena's deletion/privacy architecture — deleted imagery
must not persist through a secondary presentation layer. **Blocking** follows the
established access boundary; no cached UI continues exposing counterpart imagery
where policy requires removal.

## 17. No marketplace behavior

- **No attractiveness ranking.** No member-facing attractiveness score or
  ranking, and no visual treatment that encodes an internal desirability
  hierarchy.
- **No beauty optimization loop.** No engagement-driven photo replacement, no
  "this photo performs 32% better", no A/B attractiveness optimization, no
  popularity-driven primary-photo selection.
- **Photo feedback**, if ever built, addresses presentation clarity rather than
  beauty scoring. Not required for V1.

## 18. Diversity and accessibility

Photography accommodates broad variation in age, body type, gender expression,
disability, ethnicity, style, environment and culture. The design system assumes
no single visual model of attractiveness.

Accessibility requires useful alt treatment, screen-reader context, controls that
do not depend on the image alone, sufficient touch targets and keyboard
navigation. **Do not generate speculative descriptions of sensitive physical
attributes merely to create alt text** (D-45).

## 19. Photography across the relationship lifecycle

- **Focus Mode.** No new candidate photography while a member is in Relationship
  Focus Mode; the visual world supports the relationship state.
- **Relationship support.** Counterpart imagery is not decorative wallpaper;
  photographs appear where contextually useful.
- **Ending.** Photography follows former-partner boundaries; the sealed F-37
  boundary remains absolute.
- **Return.** Returning members are not met with a wall of new faces; Athena
  resumes understanding and introduction deliberately.
- **Successful departure.** A couple's photographs are never used publicly or for
  marketing without explicit, separate consent. Relationship success creates no
  promotional rights.

## 20. Photography outside the member experience

Any use of real member imagery outside the product requires explicit governance
and consent; D6 creates no such permission. Generic couple stock photography must
not become Athena's brand language. Future public marketing may use human
photography selectively, emphasizing individual humanity, thoughtfulness,
connection and mature emotion rather than stereotypical romantic couple imagery.

**Empty states** never use random human stock portraits. If no person is being
introduced, Athena does not manufacture one visually.

## 21. Operational behavior

**Loading** states remain calm and operationally honest; avoid skeletons that
resemble a catalog rapidly populating with people. **Failure:** preserve the
introduction, explain the condition where necessary, provide retry — the person
does not disappear because one asset failed. **Performance:** appropriately
compressed, responsive, cached where privacy permits, sized for device
conditions, at quality high enough to feel premium. **Security:** upload
validation, re-encoding, access control, authorization and deletion continue to
apply; aesthetic redesign must not bypass the secure photo pipeline.

---

## 22. Tests

**Human presentation test.** Do I feel I am encountering a person? Did I learn
something meaningful before appearance dominated? Can I clearly experience
physical attraction? Does the person feel dignified? Am I encouraged to compare?
Does Athena recede once the person appears? Is any visual treatment secretly
communicating rank? Does it work across ages and genders? Is it accessible?
Would I be comfortable being presented this way myself?

**Five-photo test.** What does each image add to understanding this person? A
set of near-duplicates is weaker despite containing more photographs.

**Progressive revelation test.** Context precedes full appearance; appearance
arrives naturally; no artificial delay; attraction respected; no manipulated
anticipation; the person becomes central; member autonomy intact.

## 23. V1 production direction

Design direction only, subject to founder review: maximum five photographs;
polished, intentional, human, authentic presentation; one person at a time;
sequence of Athena framing → meaningful human context → primary photograph →
optional deeper exploration; attraction fully acknowledged; comparison actively
discouraged through architecture; photography large enough to experience the
human being; Athena recedes as the person becomes visible; existing secure
upload, EXIF/GPS removal, access and deletion boundaries preserved.

---

## 24. Governing principles

> Athena presents a person. She does not display inventory.
> Physical attraction is real and belongs in the experience.
> Meaning precedes full appearance.
> Progressive revelation creates context, not artificial suspense.
> One human being receives attention at a time.
> Five photographs are enough to create dimensionality without creating inventory.
> Polished humanity is preferable to manufactured perfection.
> Photography should reveal the person, not Athena's brand treatment.
> Athena never optimizes humans for marketplace performance.
> No visual treatment becomes a hidden desirability score.
> When the person appears, Athena recedes.
> Human dignity remains more important than interface novelty.

### Runtime divergences recorded (evidence only, not repaired)

- **Six-photo allowance.** `src/components/photo-uploader.tsx` caps uploads at
  six (`photos.length >= 6`, `photos.length < 6`), contradicting D-07's five.
  Repair belongs to downstream implementation.
- **No counterpart photography exists.** Introductions render no counterpart
  imagery, so no progressive-revelation sequence exists to evaluate (D-09,
  D-44); F-33 remains unimplemented in runtime.
- **Uploader is a square thumbnail grid.** Member-facing photo management uses
  a cardified `aspect-square` grid inside a bordered card — acceptable for
  self-management, but it must not become the introduction presentation pattern
  under §4 and §10.
- **Empty alt text.** Photo thumbnails render `alt=""`; no accessible
  alternative or screen-reader context strategy exists yet (§18, D-45).
- **No photo reordering control.** Photos carry a `position` column but the
  member has no reorder affordance; only primary selection exists (§16).
